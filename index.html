What this is

01 — WHAT THIS IS

Almost all of you have already had the week.

You're now running teams, chapters and whole organisations.

Mostly through screens, across borders, in three time zones at once.

Nobody trained you for that version of the job.

So this December we're handing you the front of the room.

You've already been the participant. That's the whole premise. Almost everyone coming has had the week that does something to you — the small group, the morning walks, the thing one alum called finding the flame of passion and inspiration within me, because it had been gone for a while.

The Forum hands you the other side of it. Same fifty people trusting each other far too quickly, same small group holding the week together. But the sessions are yours to run, and on the Saturday some of you take one into Hamburg.

Same people. Same care. Much bigger room.

02 — HOW IT WORKS

YOU DRIVE
THE PROGRAMME

We've built the week — the shape of it, the methods, the rhythm. You bring what goes inside it. Our job is to hold the frame, feed you well, and make sure there's room to breathe.

DAYS ONE AND TWO

THE
LEADERSHIP LAB

Two days of parallel sessions and Open Space. Bring one and run it, or just turn up to other people's. The good ones are usually about the awkward stuff: conflict you can't read over video, a team that's never been in the same room, decisions where your values and your budget flatly disagree.

THE SMALL GROUP

SOMEONE NOTICES
IF YOU GO QUIET

A few people, the same few, every day. Somewhere to put whatever the day turned up. Alumni will recognise it immediately — it's the thing that comes up in our evaluations more than anything else.

SATURDAY

ONE DAY
IN HAMBURG

We take the week into the city for The VALE Symposium, a free public day of talks and workshops, fifth edition. Some of you will run something. Plenty will just go and enjoy it. Then we all come back and pull the day apart over dinner.

CAN YOU IMAGINE

You'll write the actual programme once you're there. This is the sort of week we're building the conditions for — and a fair bit of it will be your fault.

  • A conversation by the fire that opens on values and hybrid leadership and, two hours later, is somewhere far more personal — with nobody in any hurry to end it.
  • A walk before breakfast in the December cold off the Elbe, with the one person you'd been meaning to talk to properly all week.
  • A session on the thing you can't solve. Holding a team together when you only ever meet them as a grid of squares. Somebody in that room has already tried it and will tell you honestly how badly it went.
  • Learning to build a virtual experience that isn't just another video call. Vibecoding something daft and useful with the current AI tools, or throwing that out entirely and going somewhere completely different. Whoever proposes it decides.
  • A workshop with no business working. Your org chart as interpretive dance. A dreaded conversation rehearsed as a soap opera. The week's most confidently argued terrible ideas, scored out of ten by a jury of your peers.
  • The barn at eleven at night, somebody's questionable playlist, and karaoke in four languages that nobody present speaks natively.
  • Enough unstructured time to actually get to know people. Going by eight years of evaluations, that's the part you'll still be talking about next winter.

Meals, evenings and the walk before breakfast are unstructured on purpose. Our evaluations keep telling us that's where a lot of the actual work happens.

And it doesn't stop on the Sunday. The closing day sets up an alumni network and a three-month follow-up, so whatever you decide to change at home has somebody asking about it in March.

03 — THE PLACE

GUT
SCHÖNEWORTH

FREIBURG AN DER ELBE · LANDKREIS STADE

A thatched farm estate on the Elbe, about an hour west of Hamburg. Everyone sleeps, eats and works on site for all five days. This is the part of the budget we did not economise on.

  • THE APFELSCHEUNE

    Our main room is a converted apple barn. Over 120 square metres, ceilings up to 4.3 metres, daylight on both sides, and a thatched roof that regulates its own temperature. There's a second, smaller room for when the group splits.

  • THE KITCHEN

    Chef Matthias Pape cooks regional and seasonal — Galloway beef from up the road, apple juice pressed on the estate, cake baked in the house for the afternoon break. Sixteen Gault&Millau points and three toques back in 2005. Vegetarian and vegan are on the menu as a matter of course.

  • THE QUIET

    Their own site promises you can sleep with the window open. That is the sort of quiet we're talking about. There is free WiFi in every building, for when something at home genuinely can't wait.

  • THE REST OF IT

    A beamed lounge with deep sofas, long tables outside, a garden and a beer garden. It's a working farm estate that takes guests, and you'll feel the difference the moment you come up the drive.

SATURDAY 5 DECEMBER

The Symposium day

On Saturday 5 December we take the Forum into Hamburg for The VALE Symposium: a one-day public event, free and open to anyone, now in its fifth edition. Talks, peer-led workshops, a long lunch.

Some Forum participants will run a workshop or hold a table, and Friday afternoon is partly given over to preparing for that. Plenty of others will simply go along, listen, and talk to people they've never met. Nobody is obliged to be on the programme. Afterwards we go back to the estate and take the day apart together.

It's one day of five, and it's the day the week faces outwards.

THE VALE FORUM 2026

Am I eligible?

We're looking for people who are:

Working in youth, NGO or civil society — as staff, volunteers, board members, facilitators or partners. You should have some real experience to put on the table.

Roughly 20 to 45, and living in Europe.

Curious and up for co-creating rather than being lectured at.

Comfortable in English, around B2 or above. Nobody is grading you.

VALE alumni are especially welcome and a good share of the fifty places are meant for you. You don't need a VALE history to apply, and you don't need the longest CV in the room.

THE HOSTS

About The VALE

The Value-driven Advanced Leadership (The VALE) Association is an international non-profit registered in Sweden, specialising in high-quality, nontraditional leadership education for young leaders.

Since 2018 we've run six residential courses along with seminars, online workshops and the annual Symposium. They're all built on one idea: that leadership worth having starts with knowing what you actually value.

Our mission is to support aware, skilled and value-driven young leaders for a peaceful, inclusive and sustainable world.
