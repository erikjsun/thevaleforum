What this is

01 — WHAT THIS IS

Most of you did a VALE course years ago.

You're now running teams, chapters and whole organisations.

Mostly through screens, across borders, in three time zones at once.

Nobody trained you for that version of the job.

So we're putting fifty of you in a room for five days to work it out together.

02 — HOW IT WORKS

YOU BUILD
THE PROGRAMME

We're not bringing a curriculum. You propose the sessions and you run them. Our job is to hold the frame, feed you well, and make sure the week has room to breathe.

THE SESSIONS

PEER-LED,
IN PARALLEL

You can propose a session and run it, or just turn up to other people's. Nobody is expected to arrive with something prepared. The sessions that work best are usually the ones somebody is still in the middle of at work.

SPIRIT GROUPS

THE SAME
SIX PEOPLE

A small group you stay in all week, with the same people and a spirit guide. It's where the week gets processed instead of just consumed. In our course evaluations it comes up more than anything else.

SATURDAY

ONE DAY
IN HAMBURG

We take the week into the city for The VALE Symposium, a free public day of talks and workshops now in its fifth edition. You help run it rather than sit in the audience. Then we come back to the estate and take it apart together.

CAN YOU IMAGINE

None of this is scheduled, because you haven't written it yet. But going on six years of courses, some version of it will happen.

  • A conversation by the fire that opens on values and hybrid leadership and, two hours later, is somewhere far more personal — with nobody in any hurry to end it.
  • A walk before breakfast in the December cold off the Elbe, with the one person you'd been meaning to talk to properly all week.
  • A session on the thing you can't solve. Holding a team together when you only ever meet them as a grid of squares. Somebody in that room has already tried it, and will tell you honestly how it went.
  • A workshop that builds a hybrid format live, with whatever AI tooling people actually use at work, to find out whether anything really beats a video call. (One of us wants to run this. It is not settled.)
  • Twenty minutes of feedback in your spirit group that you're still turning over next October.
  • The praise circle on the last day. We won't spoil it.

03 — THE PLACE

GUT
SCHÖNEWORTH

FREIBURG AN DER ELBE · LANDKREIS STADE

A thatched farm estate on the Elbe, about an hour west of Hamburg. Everyone sleeps, eats and works on site for all five days. This is the part of the budget we did not economise on.

  • THE APFELSCHEUNE

    Our main room is a converted apple barn. Over 120 square metres, ceilings up to 4.3 metres, daylight on both sides, and a thatched roof that regulates its own temperature. There's a second, smaller room for when the group splits.

  • THE KITCHEN

    Chef Matthias Pape cooks regional and seasonal — Galloway beef from up the road, apple juice pressed on the estate, cake baked in the house for the afternoon break. The kitchen held sixteen Gault&Millau points and three toques in 2005, and the estate won Gründerstar in 2023. Vegetarian and vegan options are on the menu, not a favour.

  • THE QUIET

    Their own site promises you can sleep with the window open. That is the sort of quiet we're talking about. There is free WiFi in every building, for when something at home genuinely can't wait.

  • THE REST OF IT

    A beamed lounge with deep sofas, long tables outside, a garden and a beer garden. It's a working farm estate that happens to take guests, not a conference hotel that happens to have fields.

HOW THE FORUM CAME ABOUT

The VALE has run six residential courses since 2018. The shape barely changes: take a group somewhere quiet for a week or two, take the pressure off, and let people do the work on each other.

What comes back in the evaluations is remarkably consistent. People talk about their spirit group. They talk about the morning walks. One person wrote that it was not just a course, but a process. Another, in 2018, described finding the flame of passion and inspiration within me, because it had been gone for a while.

Then everyone goes home and starts running things. Chapters, teams, whole organisations. And they run them across screens, borders and time zones, which is a harder job than the one most of us were trained for.

We could have run a seventh course. We're doing this instead. Fifty people who already have the experience, five days, and a programme they write themselves. One of those days is public, in Hamburg, so the thinking goes somewhere beyond the room.

If you've been part of The VALE before, this is us asking you back.

THE SYMPOSIUM DAY

On Saturday 5 December we take the Forum into Hamburg for The VALE Symposium: a one-day public event, free and open to anyone, now in its fifth edition. Talks, peer-led workshops, a long lunch.

For you it isn't a field trip. You'll have spent Friday afternoon preparing for it, and you help carry the day — running a workshop, holding a table, or being the person who makes a nervous first-timer feel welcome. Afterwards we go back to the estate and take it apart together.

It's one day of five, and it's the day the week faces outwards.

AM I ELIGIBLE?

We're looking for people who are:

Working in youth, NGO or civil society — as staff, volunteers, board members, facilitators or partners. You should have some real experience to put on the table.

Roughly 20 to 45, and living in Europe.

Curious and up for co-creating rather than being lectured at.

Comfortable in English, around B2 or above. Nobody is grading you.

VALE alumni are especially welcome and a good share of the fifty places are meant for you. You don't need a VALE history to apply, and you don't need the longest CV in the room.

TRAVEL & COSTS

The confirmation fee slides: €40 reduced, €80 standard, €120 patronage. You pick the one that reflects your situation and we don't ask why. The reduced tier exists to be used; the patronage tier exists so it can be.

The fee covers accommodation, all meals, materials and the programme. Most of the real cost is covered by Erasmus+.

Travel is reimbursed up to €250 if you live outside Germany and up to €140 if you live in Germany. Some countries require green travel, meaning train or bus, and the form will tell you if that applies to you.

Getting there: the estate is at Freiburg an der Elbe, about an hour west of Hamburg. Hamburg is a major rail hub and the airport is around 25 minutes from the centre. Full travel and reimbursement instructions go out to everyone we select.

If cost is still the thing in your way, write to info@thevale.eu before you rule yourself out.

ABOUT THE VALE

The Value-driven Advanced Leadership (The VALE) Association is an international non-profit registered in Sweden, specialising in high-quality, nontraditional leadership education for young leaders.

Since 2018 we've run six residential courses along with seminars, online workshops and the annual Symposium. They're all built on one idea: that leadership worth having starts with knowing what you actually value.

Our mission is to support aware, skilled and value-driven young leaders for a peaceful, inclusive and sustainable world.
